The Heart Presumption Just Got Bigger — Here's Who's Newly Covered

Legal News · Workers' Compensation · February 17, 2026

Legal News

California's heart presumption exists for a simple reason: the job is genuinely harder on your cardiovascular system than most, and making an officer prove a specific work-related cause for a heart condition is often functionally impossible. For years, though, the presumption's reach had gaps — and one of the more glaring ones just got closed.

AB 1125 extends the heart injury presumption to peace officers employed by the Department of State Hospitals, matching protections that had previously been limited to officers at Atascadero State Hospital alone. Under the presumption, a heart trouble diagnosis developing during employment is presumed to have arisen out of and in the course of employment for workers' compensation purposes — shifting the burden onto the employer or insurer to rebut it, rather than forcing the officer to affirmatively prove causation from scratch.

If you work as a peace officer at a Department of State Hospitals facility and you've been told in the past that the heart presumption didn't apply to your position, that's no longer accurate. This expansion puts DSH officers on the same presumptive footing as their counterparts at other facilities and agencies that were already covered.

Presumption statutes only work if people actually know they exist. If you're a DSH peace officer filing a heart-related claim, make sure your representative is citing this expanded coverage explicitly — insurers don't always volunteer that a presumption applies just because the law changed.
