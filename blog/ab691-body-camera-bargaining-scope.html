Body-Worn Camera Policy Changes Are Bargainable — Here's the Legislative Push Making That Explicit

Legal News · PERB · May 15, 2026

Legal News

California's body-worn camera framework, most recently addressed through Senate Bill 691, continues to intersect with a well-established line of PERB precedent holding that surveillance and recording policies touching potential employee discipline are within the scope of representation and subject to meet-and-confer obligations.

PERB has repeatedly found that decisions to install or expand video surveillance — including body-worn cameras — implicate mandatory bargaining subjects once the footage can be used for disciplinary purposes, even where the decision to deploy the technology itself is a nonnegotiable managerial call. The distinction tracks a broader pattern in PERB's scope-of-representation analysis: the underlying policy decision may belong to management, but the disciplinary and evidentiary consequences of that decision are squarely bargainable.

For police unions in particular, this matters as departments continue expanding BWC deployment, retention policies, and integration with use-of-force review processes. Changes to when cameras must be activated, how footage factors into discipline, and how long footage is retained are exactly the kind of implementation details that trigger bargaining obligations under existing PERB case law.

If your department is rolling out BWC policy changes without bringing your union to the table first, that's worth flagging immediately — PERB's precedent on this issue is consistent enough that a unilateral change claim has real teeth.
