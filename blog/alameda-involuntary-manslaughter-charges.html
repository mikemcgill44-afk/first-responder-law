Three Officers Were Charged With Manslaughter Over a Restraint Death. Here's What the Filing Actually Alleges.

Legal News · Criminal Law · January 22, 2025

Legal News

People v. Fisher, Leahy, and McKinley (Alameda County Superior Court) put the question of restraint-related in-custody deaths squarely in front of a criminal jury. Alameda County District Attorney Pamela Price charged former officers James Fisher, Cameron Leahy, and Eric McKinley with involuntary manslaughter in the April 2021 death of Mario Gonzalez, who died after being pinned face-down during a welfare-check encounter.

The case is part of a broader pattern of prosecutors revisiting in-custody death cases involving prone restraint — a technique long flagged by medical experts and use-of-force trainers as carrying positional asphyxia risk, particularly for extended periods or when combined with body weight compression.

For officers and departments, the case underscores that training bulletins and department policy on prone restraint duration aren't just liability paperwork — they're increasingly the evidentiary backbone prosecutors point to when arguing an officer knew or should have known the risk. Departments that haven't updated restraint training to reflect current medical guidance are exposing their officers to exactly this kind of downstream criminal risk.

If your department's restraint policy hasn't been reviewed against current positional asphyxia guidance in the last few years, that's worth raising with command staff now — not after an incident forces the conversation.
