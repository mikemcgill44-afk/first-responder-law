A $3.5 Million Verdict Just Got Wiped Out Because the Jury Was Handed the Wrong Rulebook

Legal News · Wrongful Death · February 19, 2026

Legal News

Get the jury instructions wrong in a deadly force case, and it doesn't matter how the trial actually went — the whole verdict can come apart on appeal. That's exactly what happened to a $3.5 million wrongful death judgment against the City of Buena Park.

The underlying case traces back to an August 19, 2019 incident in Fullerton. Officers Bobby Colon and Jennifer Train encountered a Range Rover with expired tags that had been reported stolen. When Officer Colon moved to arrest the driver, 19-year-old David Sullivan reversed the vehicle into a passing car, fled on foot, then turned back toward the officers — who opened fire. Sullivan's mother, Deanna Sullivan, sued for wrongful death and won a $3.5 million verdict at trial.

The Court of Appeal reversed. The problem wasn't the facts — it was the law the jury was told to apply. AB 392, which reshaped California's deadly force standard under Penal Code section 835a, took effect in 2020. The 2019 shooting predated it, meaning the case should have been evaluated under the standard that existed at the time of the incident, not the more restrictive framework the Legislature enacted the following year. Instructing the jury on a standard that wasn't yet law at the time of the shooting was reversible error.

The lesson here cuts both directions. For plaintiffs' attorneys and defense counsel alike, the exact effective date of California's evolving deadly force framework isn't a technicality — it's outcome-determinative. And for officers and departments navigating pre-2020 incidents still working through the litigation pipeline, this reversal is a reminder that the correct legal standard is the one that existed on the date of the incident, not the one in effect by the time the case finally reaches a courtroom.
