CAL FIRE Told Grieving Families There Was No State Death Benefit. Fourteen Families Say That Was a Lie.

Legal News · Wrongful Death · June 22, 2026

Legal News

Losing a family member in the line of duty is devastating enough without discovering, years later, that the agency they died serving may have steered you away from benefits you were actually owed. That's the core allegation in a lawsuit brought by fourteen families of contracted air tanker pilots killed while fighting California wildfires.

The suit alleges CAL FIRE intentionally misrepresented to survivors that the federal Public Safety Officers' Benefits program was the only death benefit available to them — when California's own Public Resources Code required the state to pay an amount equal to the federal PSOB benefit, plus additional funds equal to a mid-career CAL FIRE firefighter's annual salary. According to the complaint, CAL FIRE executives made these representations knowing them to be false, fully aware of the state benefit's existence at the time.

The pilots named in the case worked either directly for contractors like DynCorp under agreements to fly the state's air tanker fleet, or for companies contracted through the U.S. Forest Service — meaning their families were navigating an already-complicated web of state and federal benefit systems even before any alleged misrepresentation came into play. The families are seeking more than $4 million collectively.

Whatever the litigation ultimately establishes about intent, the underlying gap it exposes is worth every fire agency's attention: contracted pilots and personnel occupy a benefits landscape that's genuinely confusing, and grieving families are in no position to independently audit whether they're being told the full picture. If your department relies on contracted aviation personnel, this is a moment to make sure your own death benefit disclosures are complete, accurate, and don't leave survivors to find out the hard way what they were actually owed.
