A Discipline Case Went Through Termination, Reinstatement, and Termination Again — Here's How PERB Ruled

Legal News · PERB · March 4, 2025

Legal News

California City Police Officers Association v. City of California City (PERB Dec. No. 2944-M, Feb. 26, 2025) traces a genuinely unusual procedural history. Three officers were terminated in 2019 following a use-of-force incident, reinstated in 2021 after PERB found the City had skipped required disciplinary review steps, then terminated again in 2023 — this time by the City Manager rather than the Police Chief, contrary to the department's own policy manual.

The union filed a second unfair practice charge, arguing the deviation from the policy manual's chain-of-command requirement was itself an unlawful unilateral change to disciplinary procedure. An administrative law judge agreed. PERB reversed on appeal.

The Board's reasoning turned on timing: because the officers had already waived their Skelly hearing rights before the second round of termination notices went out, there was no additional process left for the deviation to have interfered with. Had the officers not waived those rights, PERB signaled its analysis would likely have gone the other way — a deviation from established Skelly procedure is exactly the kind of change that would reasonably be viewed as significant and adverse.

The lesson for officers navigating discipline appeals: procedural rights have to be affirmatively preserved at each stage. Waiving a hearing right to move faster toward arbitration can carry consequences well beyond the immediate proceeding.
