CalPERS Quietly Raised the Bar for Approving Your Disability Retirement

Legal News · CalPERS · September 12, 2023

Legal News

Starting in 2023, CalPERS's revised local safety disability retirement review process began requiring contracting agencies to submit substantially more supporting documentation before the system will certify an application for industrial disability retirement, ordinary disability retirement, or a continuing-eligibility re-evaluation. The change didn't require new legislation — CalPERS implemented it administratively, which means most members only found out about it when their own application suddenly took longer or came back with a request for more records.

Under the new approach, agencies are expected to provide a more complete file up front: detailed medical documentation connecting the claimed disability to the member's specific job duties, a clearer administrative record of how the agency itself evaluated the claim, and more explicit justification when an agency's own determination differs from what the treating physicians concluded. CalPERS has framed this as quality control on decisions it's ultimately accountable for; from the member side, it has functioned as a new layer of friction between filing and approval.

The practical effect has been longer processing times and, in a meaningful number of cases, additional information requests that effectively restart the clock on a claim. Agencies that aren't prepared for the heavier documentation burden have sometimes passed that delay directly on to the member, since it's the agency's certification package — not the member's own filing — that has to satisfy the new standard.

Police and fire personnel applying for disability retirement now benefit from front-loading medical and job-duty documentation as thoroughly as possible, rather than assuming a straightforward claim will move quickly through an agency's standard process. Given how much of the new scrutiny falls on the agency's own paperwork, member-side pressure to keep that file complete and moving is often what keeps a legitimate claim from stalling.
