CalPERS Just Made It Harder to Get — Or Deny — A Safety Member's Disability Retirement

Legal News · CalPERS · August 24, 2026

Legal News

If your disability retirement application is sitting on your agency's desk right now, the paperwork requirements just changed underneath you. Under CalPERS Circular Letter 200-014-23, agencies determining whether a local safety member is substantially incapacitated from their usual duties must now submit a stack of new, agency-specific documentation to CalPERS before a disability or industrial disability retirement application can move forward — on top of everything CalPERS already required.

Previously, an agency's certification only had to check off a short list: a statement that the decision rested on competent medical opinion, confirmation the determination wasn't used as a substitute for discipline, a finding of substantial incapacitation, whether a workers' compensation claim was filed and accepted, whether the incapacity was industrial, the member's last day on payroll, potential third-party liability, the specific disabling condition, medical support for permanence lasting at least twelve months, and any advance disability payment amounts. Circular Letter 200-014-23 adds an entirely new layer on top: a Physical Requirements of Position form detailing exactly how often the member performs specific physical tasks — lifting, climbing, standing, interacting with the public — a formal job duty statement, a Physician's Report on Disability form signed by a physician answering pointed questions about which specific duties the member cannot perform and whether that's permanent, a Workers' Compensation Carrier Request form, and every medical report used to reach the determination.

CalPERS says the new forms exist to keep disability and industrial disability retirement limited to members who actually qualify, and cites Government Code sections 20221 and 21028 as its authority to demand the information, alongside Government Code sections 21232 and 21233, which restrict a disability retiree's ability to keep working while drawing benefits. But the real-world effect lands squarely on agencies: many have historically leaned on workers' compensation medical reports to support a disability retirement decision, and those reports often carry presumptions or prophylactic work restrictions that simply do not translate into the CalPERS incapacitation standard under Government Code section 21154, which puts the incapacitation determination in the agency's hands, not CalPERS's, for local safety members.

For members, this cuts both ways. More documentation means more opportunities for an agency to get the physical-duties analysis wrong, but it also means a denial can no longer rest on a thin, boilerplate certification — the physician's report has to specifically identify which job duties are off-limits and for how long, in writing, before CalPERS will even process the file. If your application is moving slowly or your agency is asking for medical records you haven't been asked for before, that's this circular letter at work, not a sign something has gone wrong with your claim.
