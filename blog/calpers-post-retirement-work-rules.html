Rehiring a Retired Deputy for the Weekend Shift Could Cost Your Agency Six Figures

Legal News · CalPERS · August 9, 2026

Legal News

Bringing back a retired officer or firefighter to cover overflow work sounds like an easy fix for a short-staffed agency, and it usually is — right up until CalPERS post-retirement work rules under the Public Employees' Retirement Law get triggered by a scheduling decision nobody thought twice about. When those rules are violated, CalPERS doesn't just issue a warning: it can demand retroactive pension contributions from both the retiree and the agency, and can require the retiree to repay every pension payment collected during the improper work period, going back as far as three years. What looks like a minor staffing shortcut can turn into a six-figure repayment demand almost overnight.

Two restrictions do most of the damage. First, a CalPERS retiree can work no more than 960 combined hours for CalPERS-contracting agencies in a single fiscal year, full stop. Second, a retiree generally needs a break in service of at least 180 days before starting any post-retirement work — though that 180-day wait is waived for a retiree who is a public safety officer or firefighter performing safety-related duties, unless that retiree accepted a retirement incentive, in which case the 180-day wait becomes mandatory regardless of the nature of the work.

There's a second, easily missed layer for retirees below their "normal retirement age" — the highest age tied to any retirement benefit formula that applied to them across their CalPERS career, not just their most recent job. Consider a deputy who retires at 52 from an agency with a 2%-at-50 formula, but who also has service credit from an earlier agency with a 3%-at-55 formula. His normal retirement age is 55, the higher of the two, even though the agency rehiring him may have nothing to do with that older formula. Because he's still below 55, he needs a genuine 60-day "bona fide separation from service" — meaning no predetermined agreement to return — before he can start any new CalPERS-agency work, safety exemption or not.

Compensation carries its own trap: a retiree filling an "extra help" or interim vacant position can be paid only the hourly rate equivalent to the top of the applicable published pay schedule, calculated by dividing the maximum monthly salary by 173.333. Holiday pay, contract overtime, and shift differentials are off the table entirely, with FLSA-required overtime as the sole apparent exception. Agencies that quietly pay a retired annuitant a shift differential because "that's what the position pays" are creating exactly the kind of violation CalPERS audits for. Before your agency brings a retiree back for even a single shift, run the numbers on hours, break-in-service timing, and pay rate — the cost of getting it wrong falls on both the agency and the retiree who trusted you to get it right.
