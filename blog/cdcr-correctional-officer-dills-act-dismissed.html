A CDCR Employee Alleged Bullying Over Extra Duties. Here's Why the Charge Didn't Survive PERB Review.

Legal News · PERB · June 5, 2026

Legal News

State of California (Department of Corrections and Rehabilitation) (PERB Dec. No. 3027-S, June 1, 2026) involves a CDCR employee, Amber Rodelander, who alleged the department violated the Ralph C. Dills Act by requiring her to perform work outside her job duties — including training her own supervisor and a subordinate — and that her manager responded to her objections with belittling and bullying conduct.

PERB's Office of the General Counsel issued a warning letter explaining that, based on the facts alleged, the claim appeared untimely and involved issues falling outside PERB's jurisdiction. Rodelander amended her charge, but OGC concluded the amendment still didn't cure the deficiencies and dismissed it. The Board affirmed on appeal.

The case highlights a recurring issue in Dills Act practice covering state correctional employees: PERB's jurisdiction over workplace conduct is narrower than a general harassment or hostile-work-environment claim might suggest, and timeliness rules can defeat an otherwise sympathetic set of facts if the charge isn't filed promptly after the employee has notice of the conduct.

Correctional officers and other state bargaining-unit employees with a labor-related grievance should get it in front of their union representative and, if necessary, PERB's office quickly — delay is one of the most common reasons legitimate-sounding claims never get past the pleading stage.
