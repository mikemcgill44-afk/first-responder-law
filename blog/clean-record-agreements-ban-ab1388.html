The 'Clean Slate' Deal Is Dead — What AB 1388 Actually Changes for Discipline Cases

Legal News · Discipline · March 17, 2026

Legal News

Assembly Bill 1388 is the reason why. For years, a disputed disciplinary finding could quietly disappear through a "clean record agreement" — a settlement where the officer accepted some outcome in exchange for the agency agreeing to treat the disciplinary record as if the whole thing never happened. As of this year, that tool is gone.

Assembly Bill 1388, signed by Governor Newsom, eliminated the ability of peace officers and their employing agencies to enter into clean record agreements to resolve disciplinary disputes, including pending appeals. Through December 31, 2025, this was a standard piece of the settlement toolkit. As of January 1, 2026, it isn't.

Why does this matter beyond the paperwork? Clean record agreements gave both sides flexibility — an agency could resolve a contested case without a drawn-out appeal, and an officer could resolve a case without a permanent black mark following them through POST reporting requirements and future background checks. Without that option, expect fewer negotiated resolutions and more disputes pushed all the way through the full appeal process, because the middle-ground exit ramp just got closed.

There's a real strategic shift here for anyone currently in the settlement-discussion phase of a discipline case. Whatever gets negotiated now has to live with the record intact — no quiet erasure down the line. That changes the calculus on what an acceptable settlement even looks like, and it puts more pressure on getting the underlying facts right the first time, since there's no clean-record escape hatch waiting at the end.

Anyone with a pending disciplinary matter should be talking to their representative now about how this changes the settlement landscape — the old playbook doesn't apply anymore.
