A Fire Department and Its Union Filed Charges Against Each Other Over Job Specs. PERB Dismissed Both.

Legal News · PERB · September 22, 2025

Legal News

City of Compton (PERB Dec. No. 2988-M, Sept. 17, 2025) is a case study in how quickly parallel bargaining tracks can generate conflicting unfair practice charges. The City of Compton and the Compton Firefighters Association, IAFF Local 2216, were negotiating at two separate tables simultaneously — one over a successor MOU, the other over proposed job specification changes for firefighter applicants.

When the City declared impasse in the MOU negotiations, the Association refused to keep negotiating the job specification changes at the other table. The City filed an unfair practice charge alleging the Association's refusal violated the MMBA. After the City then unilaterally approved the job specification changes, the Association filed its own charge alleging the City had failed to exhaust its bargaining obligation.

PERB's administrative law judge held a consolidated hearing and dismissed both charges, and the Board affirmed on appeal in a non-precedential decision. Neither side's conduct crossed the line into an unfair practice under the specific facts presented.

For fire unions and municipal employers navigating simultaneous negotiations on separate issues, the case is a reminder to keep the two tracks procedurally distinct — declaring impasse on one subject doesn't automatically excuse either party from continuing good-faith bargaining on an unrelated one.
