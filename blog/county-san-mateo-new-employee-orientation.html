A County Held New-Employee Orientations Without the Union in the Room. PERB Said That Violated the Law.

Legal News · PERB · August 1, 2026

Legal News

County of San Mateo (PERB Dec. No. 3044-M, July 24, 2026) addresses a right that matters for every public-sector union, including police and fire bargaining units: access to new employees during onboarding. Service Employees International Union Local 521 alleged the County violated both the Public Employee Communication Chapter (PECC) and the MMBA by holding new-employee orientations without giving the union the required ten days' advance notice, without negotiating the structure of the union's access, and without providing access at all.

An administrative law judge found the County violated PECC section 3556(a) and PERB regulations by failing to provide the required notice, and separately found the County interfered with employees' rights to be represented by Local 521 by denying access to orientations altogether. PERB's full Board affirmed on the County's exceptions.

New-employee orientation access has become an increasingly contested issue statewide since PECC's enactment, precisely because it's the first — and sometimes only — moment a new hire hears directly from their union before workplace habits and assumptions are set.

Public safety unions negotiating orientation access language in their MOUs should treat this decision as reinforcement that the notice and access requirements have real teeth, not just aspirational language departments can quietly ignore.
