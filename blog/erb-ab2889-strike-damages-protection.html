LA City and County Just Got the Same Strike Protections Every Other California Public Employer's Unions Already Had

Legal News · LA City ERB · September 25, 2024

Legal News

Assembly Bill 2889 (2024) fixed what turned out to be a consequential drafting error. A statute enacted more than a decade earlier had protected public-sector unions from being ordered to pay an employer's strike-preparation costs and related damages following a strike — but the City of Los Angeles Employee Relations Board and the Los Angeles County Employee Relations Commission were never expressly named in that statute's text.

That omission meant unions operating under ERB's and ERCOM's jurisdiction technically lacked a protection every other California public-sector union covered by PERB had enjoyed for years. AB 2889 closes that gap explicitly, extending the strike-damages protection to unions under both LA City ERB's and LA County ERCOM's jurisdiction.

The bill also addresses a separate but related jurisdictional question: it gives PERB exclusive initial jurisdiction over requests for injunctive relief seeking to enjoin organizing activity or a strike involving the City or County of Los Angeles, rather than leaving that question to be resolved in the trial courts on a case-by-case basis.

For LAPD, LAFD, and other city and county unions, this legislative fix matters most in the context of any future job action — it removes a legal vulnerability that, until 2024, could have exposed a union to damages claims other California public employees never had to worry about.
