Serving as an Officer's Board of Rights Defense Rep Is Protected Conduct Under the ERO

Legal News · LA City ERB · October 2, 2025

Legal News

Police Dept. (ERB Dec. No. U-87, 1989) protects a specific and important role in LAPD's internal discipline system: the defense representative who assists a fellow officer through a Board of Rights proceeding. The Board held that serving in this capacity constitutes protected conduct under the Employee Relations Ordinance.

The protection matters because Board of Rights defense representatives are often themselves rank-and-file officers or union representatives, taking on a role that can put them at odds with department command during a contested disciplinary proceeding. Without this protection, officers might reasonably hesitate to serve as defense reps for fear that vigorous advocacy on a colleague's behalf could invite retaliation against their own careers.

The ruling fits within ERB's broader pattern of protecting representational activity broadly — the Board has separately held that an employee's right to self-representation, questions about workplace safety, and even complaints about one's own performance evaluation all qualify as protected conduct under the ERO's interference provisions.

Officers considering whether to serve as a defense representative for a colleague facing discipline should know this role carries real legal protection. Any adverse action that appears connected to having served in that capacity is worth raising with your union immediately.
