LAFD Changed How It Fills Promotional Vacancies Without Bargaining First. Here's Why That Was Unlawful.

Legal News · LA City ERB · June 18, 2025

Legal News

Fire Dept. (ERB Dec. No. U-145, 2001) addresses a question that resurfaces every time a department restructures its promotional ladder: does the procedure for filling a new promotional classification have to be bargained? The Board's answer was yes — because of the direct effect on terms and conditions of employment, procedures for filling vacancies in a newly created promotional class fall within the scope of representation under the Employee Relations Ordinance.

The decision matters for LAFD personnel navigating promotional processes because it draws a clear line: while the department retains discretion over whether to create a new rank or classification in the first place, the mechanics of who gets considered, how vacancies get posted, and what criteria apply are not unilateral management decisions once a union has a legitimate interest in those procedures.

This precedent connects to the ERB's broader impasse-procedure framework — once a department and union have bargained a promotional process to impasse or agreement, the department is free to implement something consistent with its final offer, but it can't simply skip that process because a new rank makes the old MOU language technically inapplicable.

Fire personnel eyeing a promotional opportunity in a newly created classification should ask their union representative whether the department bargained the selection procedure — if it didn't, that's a viable unfair practice claim independent of any individual promotion decision.
