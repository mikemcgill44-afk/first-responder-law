LAFD Removed Firefighters From the Risk Management Program. The City Still Had to Bargain the Effects.

Legal News · LA City ERB · January 9, 2026

Legal News

Fire Dept. (ERB Dec. No. U-107, 1994) illustrates a distinction that trips up departments constantly: the difference between a decision itself being non-bargainable and the effects of that decision being fully bargainable. The Board found that removing unit members from the department's Risk Management Program was a sufficiently fundamental managerial decision to fall outside the direct scope of representation — but that didn't end the inquiry.

Because the removal had real consequences for affected firefighters' benefits and working conditions, the department still owed the union effects bargaining over how that removal would be implemented and what it meant for the employees involved, even though the underlying policy decision itself remained within management's discretion.

This effects-bargaining doctrine shows up repeatedly across ERB's decisions — implementation of a new custodial program, layoffs, and centralization of employee discipline have all been treated the same way: the fundamental decision belongs to management, but the downstream impact on employees has to go through the bargaining table.

When your department announces a program change framed as a pure management prerogative, don't assume that ends the conversation. Ask your union representative whether the effects of that change — not the decision itself — have been properly bargained.
