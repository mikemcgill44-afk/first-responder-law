LAPD Learned the Hard Way That You Can't Implement New Terms Before Impasse Procedures Are Exhausted

Legal News · LA City ERB · January 15, 2025

Legal News

Police Dept. (ERB Dec. No. U-74, 1988) remains one of the foundational rulings governing how far a city department can go before formal impasse procedures under the Employee Relations Ordinance are complete. The Board held that implementing changed terms and conditions of employment before exhausting the ERO's impasse process is unlawful per se — meaning no case-by-case balancing is required once the premature implementation is established.

The rule tracks the reasoning California courts have applied to the Meyers-Milias-Brown Act generally: allowing an employer to jump ahead of impasse procedures would gut the entire negotiating framework, since there would be no incentive to bargain in good faith if management could simply implement its preferred terms whenever talks stalled.

A narrow exception exists where time is genuinely of the essence, or where the employer gave the union clear notice of its intended action with enough lead time for a reasonable bargaining opportunity before implementation. Neither exception is easy to establish, and the Board has applied them narrowly in the decades since.

For LAPD and LAFD units navigating a stalled MOU negotiation, this rule remains one of the sharpest tools available: if the department moves to implement new terms before impasse procedures run their course, that's a straightforward unfair practice claim, not a close call.
