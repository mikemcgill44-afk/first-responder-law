LAFD's Union President Says the Mayor Retaliated Against Him for Criticizing Wildfire Staffing. He's Suing.

Legal News · LA City ERB · May 6, 2026

Legal News

The claim by Freddy Escobar against Mayor Karen Bass and the City of Los Angeles (2026) puts a very public test in front of the City's Employee Relations Ordinance protections for union speech. Escobar, president of United Firefighters of Los Angeles City, alleges the Mayor's office launched a retaliation campaign against him after he told reporters following the Palisades fire that the fire chief lacked the resources to staff what the incident required.

The claim alleges that after Escobar's public criticism, the Mayor's office directed LAFD leadership to abandon its standard response to questions about staffing shortages and instead claim the department had launched a comprehensive review and overhaul of procedures — a review the complaint alleges never actually existed. The city now has 45 days to respond to the administrative claim before Escobar can proceed to a lawsuit.

Escobar's position is complicated by his own separate suspension from the union earlier in 2025 following a forensic audit that found undocumented union spending — a fact the City will likely lean on in disputing the retaliation narrative. But the underlying legal question is a clean one under the ERO: can a union leader be exposed to institutional retaliation for public statements criticizing department staffing and resources, made in his capacity as an elected union representative?

The ERB decision index has long held that protected conduct under the Employee Relations Ordinance isn't limited to formal grievance activity — advocacy and public commentary tied to a union role has repeatedly been found protected. Whether this claim ultimately reaches ERB or proceeds as a standalone lawsuit, it's a live test of exactly how far that protection extends when the criticism lands on the mayor's desk.
