LAPD Cancelled a Scheduled Arbitration Hearing Without the Union's Sign-Off. The Board Called That Unlawful.

Legal News · LA City ERB · April 10, 2025

Legal News

Police Dept. (ERB Dec. No. U-205, 2009) addresses a scenario that still comes up in grievance practice: what happens when a department simply cancels an arbitration hearing it no longer wants to attend. The Board held that unilateral cancellation of a scheduled arbitration hearing, over the union's objection and without the arbitrator's own approval, is an unlawful repudiation of the MOU's grievance and arbitration provisions.

The ruling reflects a broader principle running through ERB's case law: once parties have agreed to a grievance and arbitration procedure in their MOU, an employer doesn't get to unilaterally decide the process is inconvenient partway through. The negotiated procedure has to be followed unless there's a valid affirmative defense — skepticism about the grievance's merits or arbitrability isn't one.

This matters in practice because grievance timelines are unforgiving, and a cancelled hearing can functionally kill a case if the union doesn't immediately object and preserve the record. Officers and union representatives should treat any employer-initiated cancellation of a scheduled arbitration as a red flag requiring immediate written objection.

The remedy for this kind of violation typically includes an order to proceed with the original arbitration and restoration of whatever ground was lost by the delay — but only if the union acts quickly enough to preserve the claim.
