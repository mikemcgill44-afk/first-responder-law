How Long Does a Workplace Practice Have to Exist Before the City Can't Change It Without Bargaining?

Legal News · LA City ERB · February 20, 2026

Legal News

Police Dept. (ERB Dec. No. U-91, 1991) addresses one of the more fact-dependent questions in labor practice: when does an informal, unwritten workplace habit become a protected past practice that a department can't change without bargaining? The Board's standard is that a practice cannot be brief or sporadic — it has to exist for long enough that it becomes a legitimate expectation of employees.

That standard sounds simple but gets litigated constantly, because there's no bright-line number of years or repetitions. What matters is regularity and consistency: a practice followed sporadically or with frequent exceptions doesn't rise to the level of an unwritten status quo, while one followed predictably and without deviation over time does.

Related ERB precedent has since clarified the timeliness rules connected to past-practice claims: when a department changes an unwritten practice unilaterally, the clock for filing an unfair practice charge doesn't start running until the union has actual or constructive notice of the change — not necessarily the date the change technically took effect.

If your unit has an informal but consistent practice around scheduling, assignment rotation, or similar day-to-day conditions, document how long it's been followed and how consistently. That history is exactly what would support a past-practice claim if the department tries to change it unilaterally.
