LAPD Barred Officers From Discussing an Ongoing Investigation With Each Other. The Board Upheld It.

Legal News · LA City ERB · August 14, 2025

Legal News

Police Dept. (ERB Dec. No. U-169, 2004) sits at the intersection of two competing interests every internal affairs process has to navigate: an employee's protected right to discuss workplace matters with coworkers, and a department's legitimate need to prevent witnesses from coordinating stories during an active investigation. The Board sided with the department.

The ruling held that prohibiting employees from discussing events related to a pending personnel investigation, while that investigation remains open, is a valid business justification that defeats what would otherwise be a straightforward interference claim under the Employee Relations Ordinance. The Board's reasoning centered on the integrity of the investigative process itself — allowing unrestricted discussion among potential witnesses risks contaminating the fact-finding before it's complete.

This isn't an unlimited license for departments to impose broad gag orders on any topic, however. The precedent is specifically tied to discussion of the investigation's substance while it remains pending — general restrictions unconnected to a legitimate investigative interest don't get the same deference.

Officers under investigation who are instructed not to discuss the matter with coworkers should understand this instruction generally has legal footing, at least for the duration of the investigation. That said, it doesn't override an officer's separate right to consult with union representation or personal counsel about the same matter.
