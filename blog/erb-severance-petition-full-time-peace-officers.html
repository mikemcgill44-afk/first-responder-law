Full-Time Peace Officers Have a Statutory Right to Their Own Bargaining Unit — Here's the Case That Confirmed It

Legal News · LA City ERB · November 20, 2025

Legal News

City of L.A. (ERB Dec. No. 161, 1982) established a rule that continues to shape how LAPD-adjacent classifications organize for bargaining purposes. The Board held that because full-time peace officers possess a statutory right to a bargaining unit exclusive to peace officers, carving out a separate unit of such employees from a broader unit that also includes non-peace-officer classifications is appropriate — even where the broader unit had previously functioned without issue.

The Board has also drawn a companion line on what counts as a full-time peace officer for this purpose: security personnel spending no more than 20 percent of their time on traditional law enforcement functions like deterrence, surveillance, investigation, arrest, and detention don't qualify, and aren't entitled to the separate unit protection.

This distinction matters for classifications that sit near the boundary — community service officers, certain specialized investigators, and hybrid roles that combine law enforcement duties with administrative functions all raise the question of whether the statutory peace-officer bargaining right applies.

Any classification considering a severance petition to break away from a mixed bargaining unit should start with a clear-eyed accounting of what percentage of actual job duties involve core law enforcement functions — that threshold, more than the job title, is what determines whether this precedent applies.
