ERCOM's 2022 Ruling on Oversight Subpoena Power Is Still Shaping County Labor Disputes Today

Legal News · ERCOM · November 14, 2022

Legal News

ERCOM's November 2022 decision arose from a January 2020 County ordinance conferring subpoena power on the Civilian Oversight Commission and the Inspector General — a significant expansion of investigative authority over LASD that ALADS argued was implemented without the required labor process. ALADS filed a charge alleging the County violated the ERO by adopting the ordinance without first meeting and conferring about its effects on represented deputies.

ERCOM agreed, finding the County had failed to negotiate the effects of the legislation with ALADS as required, and ordered the County to the bargaining table with an explicit expectation that negotiations would wrap up within 60 days of commencement. The commission went further, ruling that deputies were not required to respond to subpoenas issued under the new ordinance until the meet-and-confer process was complete.

This decision has since become a load-bearing piece of precedent for county labor disputes involving oversight-body authority, cited directly by the Court of Appeal in the 2024 ALADS gang-investigation case and again invoked in the 2026 ALADS/PPOA settlement over COC subpoenas of personnel records.

The throughline across all three matters is consistent: whenever LA County expands the investigative reach of civilian oversight bodies into represented employees' personnel matters, ERCOM has repeatedly required effects bargaining first — a pattern any county law enforcement union should keep in its back pocket when a new oversight authority gets proposed.
