An AFSCME Local Took the County's Children's Services Department to ERCOM. The Case Shows How the Process Works.

Legal News · ERCOM · October 1, 2019

Legal News

UFC 002-19, AFSCME Local 1083 v. Community and Senior Services Department (ERCOM, 2019) is an older matter, but it remains a useful illustration of ERCOM's standard case-processing path — one that applies just as much to law enforcement and fire bargaining units as it does to any other county department.

The case proceeded through the commission's normal sequence: a charge filed by the union, assignment to a hearing officer for fact-finding, and ultimately a commission-level Decision and Order resolving the dispute on the merits. The published decision remains part of the commission's public record and continues to be cited in county labor practice as an example of how department-level disputes get resolved when informal negotiation breaks down.

For newer union representatives navigating an ERCOM charge for the first time, older decisions like this one are worth reviewing precisely because they show the full arc of a case — not just the legal holding, but the procedural rhythm of how long each stage tends to take and what kind of factual record the hearing officer develops before the commission rules.

Whether you're representing a sheriff's deputy, a firefighter, or any other county-represented employee, understanding this procedural path in advance can help set realistic expectations for how long an ERCOM charge takes to resolve from filing to final order.
