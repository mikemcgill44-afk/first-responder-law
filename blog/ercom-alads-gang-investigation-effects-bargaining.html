LA County Ordered Deputies to Answer Questions About Gang Tattoos. A Court Said the County Had to Bargain First.

Legal News · ERCOM · November 25, 2024

Legal News

Association for Los Angeles Deputy Sheriffs v. County of Los Angeles (2024) 106 Cal.App.5th ___, is one of the most significant published decisions to touch ERCOM's jurisdiction in years. The case arose after the County's Office of Inspector General sent letters to 35 Los Angeles Sheriff's Department deputies, directing them to sit for interviews about law enforcement gang affiliation, display leg and arm tattoos, and provide photographs — all under new Penal Code sections requiring agencies to root out participation in law enforcement gangs.

ALADS filed an unfair labor practice charge with ERCOM and separately sought a court injunction, arguing the County had to meet and confer over the effects of the interview directive before implementing it. The trial court agreed, enjoining the interviews until the County completed effects bargaining or ERCOM resolved the underlying charge. The Court of Appeal affirmed, distinguishing between the OIG's non-negotiable decision to investigate — mandated directly by state law — and the negotiable effects of how that investigation would be carried out, including its close connection to potential discipline and POST decertification referrals.

The court leaned heavily on a November 2022 ERCOM decision finding the County had similarly failed to negotiate the effects of a related oversight ordinance, treating ERCOM's expertise as persuasive authority even though the commission's decisions aren't binding on the courts. That earlier ERCOM ruling had given the County an explicit 60-day benchmark for completing similar negotiations — a timeline the appellate court cited approvingly.

For LASD personnel, the case confirms that even statutorily mandated investigations aren't a blank check for implementation without bargaining. The decision to investigate belongs to the County; the manner of implementation — including anything tied to discipline, decertification, or Brady list referrals — remains squarely on the bargaining table.
