LA County Admitted It Violated the Law When It Subpoenaed Deputies' Personnel Files Without Bargaining

Legal News · ERCOM · April 24, 2026

Legal News

The settlement in UFC 031-25 and UFC 029-25 (ERCOM, 2026) resolves a dispute that began when the Civilian Oversight Commission issued subpoenas duces tecum to the Sheriff's Department in February 2025, seeking confidential personnel records belonging to members of both the Association for Los Angeles Deputy Sheriffs and the Professional Peace Officers Association. Neither union had been consulted before the subpoenas went out.

ALADS and PPOA each filed unfair practice charges alleging the County violated sections of the Employee Relations Ordinance requiring effects bargaining before implementing this kind of records demand. Rather than litigate the consolidated charges before a hearing officer, the parties reached a settlement in which the County expressly admitted it violated the ERO.

The terms were direct: the COC's February 2025 subpoenas were rescinded outright, the pending consolidated hearing was cancelled, and the parties jointly requested a formal ERCOM Decision and Order memorializing the County's admitted violation — giving both unions a documented precedent to point to if the issue recurs.

The settlement reinforces the same principle the ALADS gang-investigation case established: any county oversight body seeking confidential personnel records from ALADS- or PPOA-represented employees has to negotiate the effects of that request first, regardless of which county entity is doing the requesting.
