Negotiations With LA County Stalled? Here's What Fact-Finding and Mediation Actually Look Like at ERCOM

Legal News · ERCOM · February 11, 2026

Legal News

When negotiations between a Los Angeles County bargaining unit and the County stall short of agreement, ERCOM's impasse resolution process offers two primary tools before either side is free to take unilateral action: mediation and fact-finding. Both are formally provided for under the County's Employee Relations Ordinance, and both are administered through ERCOM.

Mediation is the less formal of the two — a neutral third party helps the parties work toward a voluntary agreement without imposing findings or recommendations. Fact-finding is more structured: a fact-finding panel reviews the parties' positions and issues written findings and recommendations for resolving the impasse, which, while not binding, carry real persuasive weight in subsequent negotiations and public opinion.

Consistent with how PERB treats the analogous process for other California public employers, ERCOM has generally treated fact-finding as a continuation of the bargaining process itself rather than a wholly separate procedure — meaning conduct during fact-finding remains subject to the same good-faith bargaining obligations that govern direct negotiations.

Sheriff's Department and Fire Department units approaching an impasse in County negotiations should understand these tools aren't just procedural formalities — a well-prepared fact-finding presentation can shift the leverage in a stalled negotiation significantly, particularly when public attention follows the panel's findings.
