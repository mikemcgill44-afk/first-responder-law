How LA County Firefighters' Bargaining Units Actually Get Structured — And Why It Matters

Legal News · ERCOM · September 30, 2025

Legal News

Los Angeles County Firefighters, IAFF Local 1014, represents thousands of Los Angeles County Fire Department employees across a range of classifications — and like any large bargaining unit, its structure hasn't stayed static over the decades. When new job classifications are created or existing ones need to be reassigned between units, ERCOM is the body responsible for approving those changes through its accretion process.

Accretion matters more than it might sound: whether a newly created classification gets folded into an existing bargaining unit, placed in a new one, or left unrepresented can shape negotiating leverage for years. A classification accreted into a large, established unit inherits that unit's bargaining strength and existing MOU protections; one left to organize independently starts from scratch.

ERCOM's general approach across accretion petitions has been to look past job titles to actual job duties and community of interest — asking whether the new or reassigned classification shares enough in common with the existing unit's working conditions, compensation structure, and job function to make joint representation sensible.

Fire personnel in a classification that's recently changed, been newly created, or sits ambiguously between existing bargaining units should raise the accretion question with Local 1014 directly — getting the unit placement right early avoids a much harder fight to reorganize representation after the fact.
