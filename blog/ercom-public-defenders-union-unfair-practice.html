LA County's Public Defenders Union Has Filed Multiple Unfair Practice Charges — Here's the Pattern

Legal News · ERCOM · August 15, 2023

Legal News

The LA County Public Defenders Union v. LA County Public Defender's Office matters — UFC 002-22 and UFC 004-23, decided by ERCOM in 2022 and 2023 respectively — reflect a recurring pattern of labor friction inside one of the County's most chronically under-resourced departments. While the Public Defenders Union doesn't represent sworn officers, its disputes with the County illustrate how ERCOM's unfair practice framework functions across the full range of county bargaining units, including those adjacent to public safety work in the courts.

Both matters proceeded to formal Decision and Order rulings from the commission, reflecting charges that didn't settle informally before reaching that stage — itself notable, since most unfair practice charges filed with ERCOM resolve short of a final commission ruling.

For any county bargaining unit, including law enforcement and fire units, the broader lesson is procedural: ERCOM's unfair practice process moves from charge, to hearing officer report, to commission review and final order — and matters that reach that final stage typically involve genuine legal disputes about scope of representation or bargaining obligations, not just workplace friction that could be resolved informally.

Public safety unions negotiating with LA County can look to the pattern in these public defender cases as a data point on how seriously ERCOM treats department-level resource and staffing disputes when they intersect with the County's bargaining obligations.
