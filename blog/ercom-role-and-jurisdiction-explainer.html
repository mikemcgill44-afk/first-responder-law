What ERCOM Actually Does — And Why It Doesn't Hear Your Discipline Appeal

Legal News · ERCOM · May 8, 2025

Legal News

The Los Angeles County Employee Relations Commission, established by Employee Relations Ordinance #9646 in 1969, is the county-level counterpart to the state's PERB for public employers not covered by PERB's jurisdiction — which, notably, includes Los Angeles County itself. Three commissioners, selected through a process laid out in the ERO, oversee the commission's work with support from an executive director and staff.

ERCOM's core functions cover a specific set of labor-relations matters: determining appropriate employee bargaining units, arranging representation elections, ruling on unfair practice charges filed against either management or employee organizations, and acting on requests for mediation, fact-finding, and impasse resolution. When a hearing officer issues a report on an unfair practice matter, the commission reviews it and issues a final Decision and Order.

What ERCOM explicitly does not do is hear disciplinary matters. An officer or deputy facing termination, suspension, or other discipline doesn't have a path to ERCOM for that dispute directly — those matters go through civil service appeal processes, Skelly hearings, and, where applicable, POBRA procedures instead. ERCOM's jurisdiction is limited to the labor-relations side of the employment relationship: bargaining, representation, and unfair practices connected to those functions.

Understanding this jurisdictional line matters because filing something with the wrong body wastes time an employee facing discipline often doesn't have. If your dispute is about representation, bargaining, or an employer's failure to negotiate a change affecting your unit, ERCOM is the right venue. If it's about the discipline itself, it isn't.
