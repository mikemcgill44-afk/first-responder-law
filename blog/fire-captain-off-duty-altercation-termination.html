He Struck His Brother at a Birthday Party — And Lost His Captain's Bars Over It

Legal News · Discipline · July 8, 2026

Legal News

A fire captain's decades of discipline-free service didn't save his job after he threw a punch at his own brother during a family birthday party, in a case that shows just how far off-duty conduct can reach into an on-duty career when the person doing the hitting outranks everyone watching. The termination appeal turned on a single disputed fact — who struck first — and the captain lost on it, despite raising a self-defense argument built around protecting his wife.

The incident happened at a colleague's home, at a party attended by friends, family, and other fire department personnel, including the captain's brother, who also worked for the department and with whom the captain had a strained relationship. During the party, the brother made a derogatory comment about the captain's wife. The captain responded by striking him. The city determined the captain's conduct violated multiple department policies governing proper conduct and good order, and terminated him following a pre-disciplinary meeting.

On appeal, the captain argued his brother wasn't a credible witness given inconsistent statements at the party and unrelated prior incidents, and claimed he struck his brother only in defense of his wife, whom he said was in imminent physical danger. The hearing officer found that even setting aside the brother's credibility, testimony from other witnesses at the party established that the captain struck the first blow while his brother was turned away from him — a detail flatly inconsistent with a claim of defending against an active physical threat. The hearing officer also emphasized that the altercation occurred in front of members of the public, including young children.

The termination stood on a theme that recurs throughout fire and police discipline cases alike: supervisory personnel are held to a higher standard of conduct precisely because of their rank, whether the misconduct happens on duty or at a private party. The hearing officer's framing was blunt — a captain throwing a punch in front of children at a social gathering is "the antithesis" of what the public expects from fire department leadership, and a long clean record does not automatically outweigh a single incident that undercuts the public trust a captain's rank is supposed to represent.
