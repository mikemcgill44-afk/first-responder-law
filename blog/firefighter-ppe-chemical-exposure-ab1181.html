Your Turnout Gear May Be Poisoning You. California Just Ordered a Fix.

Legal News · Workers' Compensation · May 26, 2026

Legal News

The same turnout gear designed to keep firefighters alive in a burning structure has come under increasing scrutiny for what it's made of, and Assembly Bill 1181 forces California's occupational safety regulators to finally do something about it. The law requires the Occupational Safety and Health Standards Board, working with the Department of Industrial Relations, to reconsider the state's existing firefighter personal protective equipment safety order by January 1, 2028.

The core problem AB 1181 targets is chemical exposure baked into the equipment itself. The Board must now use scientific research to build a PPE standard that is both genuinely protective for firefighting performance and free of hazardous substances that pose long-term health risks, with manufacturers required to certify that compliance. This isn't a recommendation — the statute directs the Board to actually modify its existing safety order to reflect updated National Fire Protection Association performance standards, rather than leaving those standards as aspirational guidance departments could choose to ignore.

The rollout is deliberately staged. Auxiliary equipment — self-contained breathing apparatuses, hearing protection, protective communication devices, and fall-protection gear — gets a later implementation date than firefighting garments themselves, meaning departments won't have to replace their entire equipment inventory on a single deadline. AB 1181 also requires that gear already in service be phased out through normal attrition or within ten years at the outside, giving departments a realistic budget runway rather than an unfunded immediate mandate. The Division of Occupational Safety and Health owes the Governor and Legislature a progress report by July 1, 2026, with that reporting requirement sunsetting in 2030.

For fire departments, this is worth getting ahead of rather than waiting on: budget conversations about PPE replacement cycles should start accounting for a compliant-standard purchase requirement well before the 2028 modification deadline actually lands. For firefighters, the law is a direct legislative acknowledgment that the gear you've been issued may carry a long-term health cost the state is only now moving to regulate.
