Coming Back From Leave and Getting Hit With a Fitness-for-Duty Order? Here's the Line the Law Draws

Legal News · Fitness for Duty · August 5, 2026

Legal News

Nothing sours a return from medical leave faster than walking back into the station and immediately being told you need to pass a fitness-for-duty exam before you can actually go back to work. It happens more often than most first responders expect, and the legal boundaries around it deserve more attention than they usually get.

The general rule tracks what applies to any employer-mandated medical inquiry: a fitness-for-duty referral issued after protected leave — whether under FMLA, CFRA, or workers' compensation-related leave — has to be job-related and consistent with business necessity. It cannot simply be a reflexive gatekeeping step triggered by the fact that leave was taken in the first place. Courts have been increasingly willing to scrutinize whether a post-leave referral is actually tied to a legitimate, documented concern about job performance or safety, or whether it functions as a pretext that discourages employees from using protected leave at all.

That distinction matters enormously for departments and for the officers and firefighters navigating a return to duty. A referral based on genuine, specific, contemporaneous concerns about fitness for the essential functions of the job is defensible. A referral that amounts to "you were out for a while, so now you have to prove yourself again" is exactly the kind of blanket policy that invites a disability discrimination or retaliation claim.

If you're returning from protected leave and get referred for a fitness-for-duty evaluation, ask for the specific, documented basis for the referral in writing before you go. If the answer boils down to "standard procedure after leave," that answer alone may not hold up.
