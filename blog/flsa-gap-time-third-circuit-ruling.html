A Federal Court Just Narrowed a Wage Claim — Here's Why California First Responders Don't Need to Panic

Wage & Hour · June 7, 2026

Wage & Hour

Call it "gap time": the straight-time hours an officer or firefighter works below the 40-hour overtime threshold during a week that also includes overtime, hours that sometimes slip through the cracks of a paycheck unpaid. Unions have leaned on federal wage law to recover that missing pay for years. A recent Third Circuit ruling just narrowed that path — but only in the states that circuit covers, and California isn't one of them.

What the Third Circuit Actually Said

In a split decision, the court held that federal wage law simply doesn't create a standalone claim for unpaid gap time. The panel majority read the statute's silence on the subject as intentional: Congress spoke clearly about minimum wage and about overtime premiums, but said nothing about straight-time pay for hours worked below 40 in an overtime week, so the court declined to read a remedy into that silence — even though the federal Department of Labor's own longstanding guidance suggests otherwise. That puts the Third Circuit in agreement with the Second Circuit, and in direct conflict with the Fourth, which reached the opposite conclusion a few years earlier.

Why California Is a Different Story

Here's the part that matters most for anyone reading this from a California department: the Ninth Circuit, which actually governs here, has never adopted the Third Circuit's rejection. Ninth Circuit precedent has left the door open, holding that while a "pure" gap time claim standing alone is a murkier question, federal law still requires that all straight-time pay for non-overtime hours be paid out before an employer's overtime premium can even be considered properly calculated. A later unpublished decision from the same court went further, confirming that gap time claims tied to overtime calculations weren't waived or barred. In plain terms: in California, unpaid straight-time hours in an overtime week can still undercut whether the employer calculated the overtime premium correctly in the first place — a live and viable argument regardless of what happened in Pennsylvania.

And California Law Doesn't Depend on Any of This Anyway

Even setting the federal question aside entirely, California public employees have their own, often stronger, protections that don't care what a Pennsylvania court decided. Unpaid straight-time wages can be pursued as violations of California's own minimum wage law, a claim that isn't touched by the Third Circuit's ruling at all. And most memoranda of understanding in the public labor relations world include "pay all hours worked" or "no off-the-clock work" language — contractual promises that get enforced through grievance arbitration and can be strengthened further through interest arbitration.

California overtime claims under state law remain genuinely difficult for many public employees because of applicable exemptions, which is exactly why preserving the gap time argument — whether as a stepping stone to a full federal overtime claim or as a state minimum-wage claim — matters as much as it does.

Bottom Line

A ruling out of the Third Circuit sounds alarming in a headline, but it doesn't reach into California. Between Ninth Circuit precedent, state minimum wage protections, and MOU language that many unions already have in hand, the pathway to recovering unpaid straight-time hours here remains very much open.
