Fired First, Disabled Second? The CalPERS Case That Says the Order Matters

Legal News · CalPERS · April 18, 2006

Legal News

Haywood v. American River Fire Protection District (1998) 67 Cal.App.4th 1292 keeps surfacing in CalPERS administrative hearings for a simple reason: it settled a question that comes up constantly when a public safety employee is disciplined and disabled around the same time. A firefighter terminated for cause tried to claim disability retirement afterward. The Court of Appeal said no, and the reasoning still controls today.

The court's logic turned on timing and cause. Where an employee is fired for misconduct unrelated to a disabling medical condition, and the termination doesn't preempt an otherwise valid, already-ripe disability claim, the firing severs the employment relationship completely — and disability retirement statutes only make sense in the context of a relationship that could theoretically be restored if the employee recovers. Once that relationship is gone for cause, there's nothing left to "retire" from.

The rule isn't absolute, and that's the part agencies and members both tend to skip over. If the disabling condition itself caused the misconduct, or if a legitimate disability claim had already matured before the discipline was initiated, Haywood doesn't automatically bar the claim. CalPERS and the courts have since drawn that distinction repeatedly in later proceedings, making the underlying medical timeline — not just the discipline timeline — the thing that actually decides these cases.

For any officer or firefighter facing simultaneous discipline and a worsening medical condition, the practical lesson is to get a disability retirement application on file the moment the condition is legitimately disabling, rather than waiting to see how a disciplinary investigation shakes out. Under Haywood, waiting can cost you the claim entirely.
