CalPERS Tried to Deny Her Pension Over a Job That Existed Only in Theory

Legal News · CalPERS · October 25, 2019

Legal News

Cari McCormick spent over a decade appraising property for Lake County out of the Lakeport courthouse before her body started rebelling against the building itself — chronic pain, fatigue, and a mysterious reaction that got worse every time she walked back in. Her employer refused to let her work anywhere else. When she applied for CalPERS disability retirement, the system denied her, arguing she could theoretically do the job somewhere else — just not at that particular courthouse. In McCormick v. California Public Employees' Retirement System (2019) 41 Cal.App.5th 428, the California Court of Appeal said that argument doesn't hold up.

CalPERS and the trial court both leaned on the idea that McCormick's doctors agreed she could perform her job duties if the "offending environment" were fixed, or if she worked somewhere else entirely. The Court of Appeal called that framing irrelevant. Under Government Code section 21156, the question isn't whether an employee could hypothetically do the job for some other employer in some other building — it's whether she can perform her usual duties for her actual employer, at the location that employer actually requires. Lake County never offered McCormick a courthouse-free alternative. It denied her request to telecommute and eventually fired her once her medical leave ran out.

The court also shut down CalPERS's fallback theory that McCormick's failure to secure a workplace accommodation should count against her. Unlike the Teachers' Retirement Law, the PERL contains no requirement that a member request or receive an accommodation before qualifying for disability retirement. Whether or not Lake County should have accommodated her was beside the point — she asked, was refused, and that refusal doesn't get held against her when CalPERS decides whether she's disabled. For any first responder whose department has quietly declined to reassign, retrain, or relocate someone after an injury, this case is the reminder that "you could theoretically do this job somewhere else" is not a lawful basis to deny a pension.
