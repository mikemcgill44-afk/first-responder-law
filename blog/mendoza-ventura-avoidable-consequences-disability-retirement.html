He Turned Down a 90% Successful Surgery — And It Cost Him His Disability Retirement

Legal News · Line-of-Duty Disability · December 3, 2025

Legal News

Ventura County Deputy Sheriff Alberto Mendoza injured his back on duty in 2014 and again in 2015. Three separate doctors recommended surgery, one predicting a 90 percent chance of a good to excellent outcome, and the county approved it. Mendoza said no. Years later, when he applied for a service-connected disability retirement, the county fought it — and in Mendoza v. Board of Retirement of the Ventura County Employees' Retirement Association, 2025 Cal. App. LEXIS 865, the California Court of Appeal sided with the county.

The case turned on the old common-law doctrine of avoidable consequences, sometimes called mitigation of damages: an application for service-connected disability retirement can be denied if the applicant's disability was caused, continued, or made worse by an unreasonable refusal to undergo appropriate medical treatment. A hearing officer found that Mendoza refused the recommended surgery, unilaterally stopped a doctor-prescribed home exercise program because it hurt, and skipped a later work-hardening program the county had authorized to help him return to duty. The retirement board adopted those findings and denied his claim; the trial court agreed.

On appeal, Mendoza argued the doctrine shouldn't apply because time had passed — the original surgery might not work as well now, and later-recommended procedures might not have restored him to full duty anyway. The Court of Appeal rejected that argument, holding that avoidable consequences applies not just when treatment would clearly return an employee to work, but also when the employee could likely have returned to work if he hadn't unreasonably refused timely treatment in the first place, even if that treatment's effectiveness has since eroded with time. In plain terms: you don't get to run out the clock on your own recovery and then point to the clock as the reason you're disabled. For any deputy, officer, or firefighter weighing whether to follow through on a recommended procedure before filing for disability retirement, this case is a hard warning that the refusal itself can become the county's whole defense.
