California Quietly Rewrote the Rules Behind Your Pension Calculation

Legal News · CalPERS · April 28, 2026

Legal News

Most officers and firefighters never look past the final number on their pension estimate, but that number is the product of a statutory formula that governs exactly how "compensation earnable" and "pensionable compensation" get calculated — and Senate Bill 853 just rewrote a meaningful chunk of that formula across three of California's major retirement systems at once. The law amends provisions spanning PERS, the county systems governed by the County Employees Retirement Law of 1937, and the State Teachers' Retirement System, all in service of aligning them more consistently with the Public Employees' Pension Reform Act of 2013.

The headline change addresses members who split their careers across systems or retire concurrently under more than one plan. SB 853 standardizes how those overlapping systems calculate compensation for purposes of a member's final retirement allowance, and clarifies that "state service" includes county employment that already qualifies as PEPRA compensation. It also directs county systems to calculate final compensation during a leave of absence based on the member's pay rate at the start of that absence — a detail that matters directly to safety members who take extended medical or disability leave before separating from service.

SB 853 also closes a door that some county prosecutors, public defenders, and investigators had been using to retroactively convert general-service time into safety service: that conversion is now limited to service performed before January 1, 2013, with anything after that date required to comply with PEPRA's safety-benefit limits. On the compliance side, the law lets county retirement boards set their own reporting intervals for rehired retirees and authorizes a $200-per-month fee against employers who fail to report or enroll those employees on time, giving boards a real enforcement tool against agencies that have been sloppy about post-retirement work reporting.

None of this changes what a given member is entitled to in principle, but it does change how that entitlement gets computed for anyone with service in more than one system, anyone converting between general and safety classifications, or anyone whose agency has been lax about rehired-retiree reporting. If your career includes a stint in a different retirement system, a classification change, or a return to work after retirement, it's worth having your specific compensation calculation reviewed against these updated rules rather than assuming your original estimate still holds.
