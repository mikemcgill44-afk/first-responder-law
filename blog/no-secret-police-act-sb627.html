California Officers Can No Longer Wear Masks on Duty Without Identifying Themselves — What SB 627 Actually Requires

Legal News · Criminal Law · January 8, 2026

Legal News

Senate Bill 627, known as the "No Secret Police Act," prohibits both federal and local law enforcement officers operating in California from wearing face masks while conducting official duties, and requires every California law enforcement agency to publicly post a written policy addressing the practice.

The companion identification requirement matters just as much for day-to-day operations: as of January 1, 2026, any law enforcement personnel not in uniform must visibly display identification showing their agency and either their name or badge number. That requirement reaches plainclothes detectives, task force officers, and anyone operating outside a standard uniform during an official encounter with the public.

The law emerged from concerns about unidentifiable federal immigration enforcement personnel operating in California communities, but its text applies broadly to law enforcement generally — meaning California agencies need policies in place regardless of whether their own personnel were the intended target of the legislation.

If your department hasn't posted a written mask policy or updated plainclothes ID procedures to reflect SB 627, that's a compliance gap worth closing immediately — both provisions took effect at the start of 2026, and the posting requirement is public-facing, meaning noncompliance is easy for the public to spot.
