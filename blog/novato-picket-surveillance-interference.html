A City Photographed Picketers Outside a Public Building. PERB Says That Alone Can Be an Unfair Practice.

Legal News · PERB · August 13, 2026

Legal News

City of Novato (PERB Dec. No. 3048-M, Aug. 12, 2026) is the most recent word from PERB on a recurring flashpoint in public-sector labor disputes: employer surveillance of picketing and protest activity. SEIU alleged the City interfered with MMBA-protected rights when its agents photographed or video recorded picketers outside a city building — or at minimum created the appearance of doing so.

PERB's Office of the General Counsel initially dismissed the charge for failing to state a prima facie case. SEIU appealed, and the Board reversed, reaffirming a principle with real weight for police and fire unions that picket or demonstrate near department or city facilities: surveillance of protected activity, or even the appearance of surveillance, tends to chill the exercise of those rights.

The Board's holding gives charging parties a workable pleading standard going forward — alleging that an employer's agent went beyond merely observing open, public protected activity and instead photographed, video recorded, or otherwise created a record of it (or the appearance of one) is enough to plead a valid interference claim.

For unions planning informational picketing or public demonstrations, this decision is worth having on hand. If department or city personnel show up with cameras during a lawful picket, document it — PERB has just made clear that conduct alone can support a charge.
