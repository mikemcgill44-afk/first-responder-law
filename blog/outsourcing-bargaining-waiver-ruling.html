Public agencies love a general management-rights clause. It's the contractual equivalent of a blank check — until a court reminds everyone that blank checks don't actually cash themselves.

That's the lesson from Los Angeles County Professional Peace Officers Association v. County of Los Angeles, certified for publication April 10, 2026. The County wanted to hand off security work at the Kenneth Hahn Hall of Administration to a private contractor, and pointed to a clause in the MOU stating management had "no obligation to negotiate the decision of any reorganization" as its ticket out of the bargaining table entirely.

The Court of Appeal wasn't buying it. Waivers of statutory bargaining rights under the Meyers-Milias-Brown Act have to be clear and unmistakable — not implied, not inferred, not squeezed out of language that was written to cover something else. The panel picked apart the MOU's transfer-of-functions article and found it addressed the consequences of a transfer, not a surrender of the right to negotiate over whether the transfer should happen at all. "Any reorganization" was vague enough to plausibly mean the County's basic Government Code section 3504 management prerogatives — not outsourcing specifically.

The takeaway for every association reviewing an MOU renewal: general reorganization or management-rights boilerplate is not a substitute for language that actually says the words "outsourcing" or "contracting out." If your contract doesn't say it in plain terms, this case says the County still owes you a seat at the table before it hands your members' work to a vendor.

PERB has already been applying this standard rigorously, and now there's a published Court of Appeal decision backing it up. Time to reread your MOU's management-rights section with fresh eyes.
