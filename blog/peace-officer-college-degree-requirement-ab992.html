Every New California Officer Will Need a College Credential — Starting in 2031

Legal News · Discipline · June 2, 2026

Legal News

California has been building toward a higher education floor for peace officers for years, and Assembly Bill 992, paired with the urgency legislation Senate Bill 385, finally locks that requirement into place without waiting on POST to separately approve the criteria first. Starting January 1, 2031, every peace officer in California must hold either a modern policing degree or a professional policing certificate, earned no later than 36 months after receiving their basic certificate from POST.

The degree track requires at least 60 semester units (or 90 quarter units), credit for POST-certified academy coursework, and instruction spanning communications, psychology, writing, ethics, and criminal justice. The certificate track is a lighter lift — a minimum of 16 semester units or 24 quarter units covering the same subject areas — but it's still a real academic requirement layered on top of academy training that didn't previously carry a college-credit component. AB 992 also authorizes specified credential evaluation services to assess foreign college degrees for equivalency, opening the requirement to officers whose prior education happened outside a U.S. institution.

Not everyone starts the clock at 36 months. Officers with at least eight years of experience as a sworn officer from another state, separated in good standing, or with at least eight years of honorable military service, get a pass on the requirement entirely. Officers with less service time in either category get an extended 48-month window instead. And anyone already enrolled in a basic academy or already employed as a peace officer as of December 31, 2030 is grandfathered out of the requirement altogether — meaning this is squarely aimed at the next generation of hires, not the current workforce.

For departments, the runway here is generous but not infinite: agencies have several years to build tuition assistance, partnerships with community colleges, or in-house certificate pathways before this becomes a retention issue for officers hired close to the 2031 line. For prospective officers weighing the career, the message is straightforward — factor a real academic commitment into your first few years on the job, because it's no longer optional once your basic certificate is in hand.
