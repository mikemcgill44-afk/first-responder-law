What You Tell Your Peer Support Group Just Got a Lot More Confidential

Legal News · Fitness for Duty · May 19, 2026

Legal News

California's Law Enforcement Peer Support and Crisis Referral Services Program has always let officers speak confidentially one-on-one with a peer support team member, but that protection had a real gap for anyone who found support in a group setting rather than an individual conversation. Senate Bill 459 closes that gap by extending the same confidentiality rules to group peer support services.

The statute redefines "confidential communications" to explicitly cover information shared between law enforcement personnel and other participants during group sessions led by a peer support team member or a mental health professional. "Group peer support services" now means any session involving at least one peer support team member or mental health professional and more than one recipient — language specifically drafted to capture the group-therapy-style format many departments have adopted as peer support programs have matured beyond one-on-one models.

With that expanded definition in place, officers now have the same right they've long had for individual peer support conversations: the right to refuse to disclose, and to prevent others from disclosing, what was said during a group session. The exceptions that already existed for one-on-one peer support carry over, with SB 459 adding juvenile delinquency proceedings to the list of settings where the confidentiality protection doesn't apply.

For departments running or considering group-format peer support, this is worth folding directly into program materials and intake disclosures, since officers deciding whether to participate in a group setting are entitled to know exactly how far that confidentiality now extends. For officers, the practical upside is straightforward: a support format that many find more sustainable than one-on-one sessions no longer comes with a smaller legal shield attached to it.
