Retiring Mid-Investigation Won't Save Your Pension Anymore

Legal News · Pensions & Retirement · May 5, 2026

Legal News

The Public Employees' Pension Reform Act of 2013 already requires any public employee convicted of a job-related felony to forfeit their accrued pension rights back to the date the felony conduct began, but that forfeiture rule only bites once there's an actual conviction — and a well-timed retirement in the middle of an investigation has historically offered a way to make the underlying inquiry quietly go away. Assembly Bill 1067 shuts that door.

Under AB 1067, if a public employer's misconduct investigation suggests the employee may have committed a crime connected to their official duties or to obtaining salary or retirement benefits, the employer must keep investigating even if the employee retires while the inquiry is still open. Retirement no longer functions as an automatic off-ramp that ends the agency's fact-finding — the statute treats the investigation as a matter separate from the employee's ongoing employment status.

AB 1067 also requires the employer to refer the matter to the appropriate law enforcement agency once the internal investigation indicates possible criminal conduct, at which point the employer may close its own investigation and let the referral proceed independently. If that referral eventually produces a felony conviction for the underlying conduct, the existing PEPRA forfeiture rule applies in full — meaning the employee forfeits all accrued pension rights and benefits back to the date the misconduct began, exactly as if they'd never left the payroll.

For agencies, this closes a real accountability gap: an employee can no longer use retirement timing as leverage to pressure an investigation toward an early, incomplete close. For employees under investigation, the lesson is equally direct — retiring does not stop the clock on a pending criminal referral, and if a conviction eventually follows, the pension consequences attach exactly as they would have if you'd stayed employed through the entire process.
