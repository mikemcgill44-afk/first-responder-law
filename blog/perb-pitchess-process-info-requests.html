A Union Can't Just Ask for Your Personnel File — PERB Confirms the Pitchess Process Still Applies

Legal News · PERB · February 6, 2025

Legal News

In Trustees of the California State University (Stanislaus) (PERB, Jan. 31, 2025), the Public Employment Relations Board addressed a question that comes up constantly in police labor practice: can a union simply request a member's confidential personnel file the way it requests any other employer information? PERB said no.

The Board held that unions cannot obtain confidential peace officer personnel records through PERB's ordinary information-request framework. Instead, those records remain subject to the Pitchess process — the statutory procedure requiring a noticed motion and in-camera judicial review before confidential officer personnel information can be disclosed, even to the officer's own union.

The decision is a rare limitation on what is otherwise a broad, well-established union right to information necessary for representational duties. PERB's reasoning leaned on the specific statutory confidentiality protections California law gives peace officer personnel records — protections that exist independently of, and in tension with, a union's general right to information under the MMBA.

For police unions, the practical effect is procedural: representational information requests touching personnel file contents now need to route through Pitchess, not a standard demand letter. Build that extra step into grievance timelines so it doesn't create an unexpected delay when a case turns on personnel history.
