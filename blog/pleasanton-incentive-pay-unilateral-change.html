A City Changed the Rules on Incentive Pay and Then Demanded Money Back. PERB Ordered It Reversed.

Legal News · PERB · July 25, 2026

Legal News

City of Pleasanton (PERB Dec. No. 3043-M, July 23, 2026) breaks down what happens when an employer quietly redefines eligibility for a pay benefit already built into the employment relationship. The Pleasanton City Employees Association, AFSCME Local 955, alleged the City violated the MMBA by changing its criteria for educational incentive pay without giving the union advance notice or an opportunity to bargain.

An administrative law judge found the City had made not one but two separate unilateral changes: first, adopting a new, stricter approach to determining incentive pay eligibility, and second, deciding to require employees to repay incentive pay they had already received under the old criteria. PERB affirmed both findings and ordered the standard remedy — a cease-and-desist order, restoration of the prior status quo, and back pay with interest.

The Board's decision reinforces a point that comes up constantly in public safety pay disputes: wages are squarely within the scope of representation, and that includes eligibility criteria for pay incentives tied to certifications, education, or specialty assignments — the kind of pay structures common in police and fire MOUs.

If your department has recently tightened eligibility for any incentive, certification, or specialty pay without formal bargaining, this decision gives your union solid ground to challenge both the change and any attempted clawback of past payments.
