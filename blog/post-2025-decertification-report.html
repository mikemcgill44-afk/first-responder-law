POST Pulled 56 Badges Last Year Over Convictions Alone — Here's What Actually Triggers Decertification

Legal News · Criminal Law · March 12, 2026

Legal News

The 2025 Peace Officer Standards and Accountability Division Annual Report from California POST lays out, in cold numbers, just how much decertification exposure now flows directly from criminal convictions. Fifty-six officers were revoked in 2025 based on felony or misdemeanor convictions under Government Code section 1029 occurring after January 1, 2022, with another 27 deemed ineligible for pre-2022 convictions.

Since SB 2 created California's certification and decertification framework, a criminal conviction doesn't just mean sentencing exposure — it means a parallel administrative track that can end a career even after the criminal case resolves. POST currently has more than 7,100 open misconduct cases in its pipeline, with roughly 3,500 under active POST investigation.

The nine categories of serious misconduct POST can act on run from dishonesty and physical abuse to sexual assault, demonstrated bias, and failure to intercede — several of which overlap directly with conduct that can also generate criminal charges. An officer facing criminal exposure needs to understand from day one that the criminal case and the certification case are not the same fight, and a resolution in one doesn't automatically resolve the other.

If you're a peace officer under investigation for conduct that could implicate both criminal charges and POST review, coordinate your defense across both tracks from the start. Waiting until after a criminal disposition to think about decertification exposure is often too late to matter.
