POST Staff Can Now See Your Criminal History — But Only After Their Own Background Check

Legal News · Civil Rights · June 16, 2026

Legal News

The Commission on Peace Officer Standards and Training already has broad authority to review misconduct investigations and access sensitive law enforcement data, but until recently, the people actually doing that work on POST's behalf weren't necessarily vetted the same way the officers whose files they're reviewing were. Assembly Bill 354 closes that gap by requiring fingerprint-based background checks for a much wider slice of the people who touch that information.

Under AB 354, any POST employee or staff member — a category that now expressly includes appointees, volunteers, contractors, and subcontractors — whose job requires access to criminal offender record information, state summary criminal history, or data drawn from the California Law Enforcement Telecommunications System must undergo a fingerprint-based state and national criminal history background check before getting that access. This isn't a one-time formality baked into a hiring packet; it's a standing eligibility requirement tied directly to the sensitivity of the information involved.

Once someone clears that background check and their duties call for it, AB 354 authorizes them to inspect or duplicate CLETS-derived information, and separately gives POST itself the authority to inspect and duplicate any criminal history, criminal offender record, or other sensitive or confidential information it determines it needs for its official duties. In effect, the law widens POST's practical reach into agency records while tightening the screening standard for the specific individuals exercising that reach — a trade-off that expands oversight capacity without expanding it to just anyone with a POST badge or contract.

For agencies fielding a records request or an inquiry from POST staff, the practical question worth asking isn't just whether POST has the authority to ask — it's whether the specific individual making the request has actually cleared the fingerprint-based background check the statute now requires. That's a new documentation point worth confirming before sensitive personnel or criminal history data changes hands.
