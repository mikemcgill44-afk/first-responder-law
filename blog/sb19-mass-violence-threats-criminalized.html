Threatening a School, Church, or Workplace Is Now Its Own Standalone Crime in California

Legal News · Criminal Law · January 5, 2026

Legal News

Senate Bill 19 gives California a dedicated criminal statute for threats of mass violence directed at workplaces, schools, houses of worship, and medical facilities — closing a gap that previously forced prosecutors to rely on more general, and sometimes poorly fitted, threat and disturbing-the-peace statutes.

For patrol officers and detectives fielding threat reports, the new statute matters because it gives responding officers a clearer charging framework when a threat targets a specific category of protected location, rather than requiring them to shoehorn the conduct into statutes written for different fact patterns. That clarity can matter at the moment of arrest, when an officer has to make a fast probable-cause call.

The law arrives against a backdrop of rising concern about targeted violence against schools and workplaces, and mirrors a broader legislative trend of carving specific-purpose criminal statutes out of general threat law when a category of harm becomes frequent and predictable enough to warrant its own charging tool.

Departments should make sure patrol briefings and detective units are up to speed on the new statute's elements, since a threat report that would previously have been screened as a lower-level offense may now support a more serious charge — with corresponding implications for bail, investigation priority, and referral.
