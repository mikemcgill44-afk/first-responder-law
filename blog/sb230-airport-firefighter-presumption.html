Airport Firefighters Just Caught Up to Everyone Else on Cancer and PTSD Coverage

Legal News · Workers' Compensation · January 20, 2026

Legal News

There's never been a good reason for a firefighter's presumption coverage to depend on whether the runway next to their station belongs to a commercial airport. SB 230 finally fixes that inconsistency.

Effective for injuries occurring on or after January 1, 2026, the law extends California's cancer presumption under Labor Code section 3212.1 and the PTSD presumption to firefighters who work at fire departments providing protection to a commercial airport — the same protections municipal and county firefighters have relied on for years, and coverage that also now reaches firefighters serving NASA installations and U.S. Department of Defense facilities.

The mechanics of the presumption haven't changed: a firefighter who develops a covered cancer or a PTSD diagnosis is presumed to have contracted it through occupational exposure or the demands of the job, shifting the burden to the insurer to rebut that connection rather than making the firefighter prove causation from a standing start. What's changed is who gets to invoke it. Airport firefighters were, until this year, carrying the same occupational exposure risks as everyone else while facing a heavier evidentiary burden to get a claim approved.

If you're an airport firefighter — or represent one — with a pending cancer or psychiatric injury claim tied to service on or after January 1, 2026, this presumption should be part of the claim from day one. Don't let an adjuster process the claim as if the old, narrower rules still apply.
