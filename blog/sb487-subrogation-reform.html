Hurt on the Job by Someone Else's Negligence? The Rules on Who Gets Paid First Just Changed

Legal News · Workers' Compensation · February 10, 2026

Legal News

Get hurt on duty because of someone else's negligence — a drunk driver at a traffic stop, a negligent contractor at a fire scene — and you're suddenly navigating two systems at once: workers' compensation and a third-party civil claim. For years, the employer's subrogation rights in that third-party recovery could eat a painful chunk of whatever settlement the injured officer or firefighter actually won. SB 487 rebalances that math.

The new law limits how much an employer's workers' compensation carrier can claw back from a third-party settlement when the available insurance isn't enough to fully compensate both the employer's comp payout and the injured employee. Under SB 487, specified peace officers and firefighters are now guaranteed at least two-thirds of available liability insurance proceeds before the employer's subrogation interest gets paid — a floor that didn't reliably exist before.

The law also closes a quieter but equally painful loophole: employers can no longer treat an employee's third-party recovery as a credit against future workers' compensation benefits. That means a settlement from the at-fault driver or contractor doesn't secretly reduce the comp benefits an injured officer or firefighter is entitled to down the road — the two systems stay separated the way they're supposed to be.

For anyone currently navigating a workers' comp claim tied to a third-party injury, this is worth flagging early with your attorney. The settlement math that applied to claims resolved before this year doesn't apply anymore, and the difference can mean tens of thousands of dollars actually landing in the injured employee's pocket instead of getting absorbed by subrogation.
