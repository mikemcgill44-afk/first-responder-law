SDPD Has Been Quietly Filing Away 4 Out of Every 5 Complaints as 'Miscellaneous.' A Lawsuit Says That's Illegal.

Legal News · Civil Rights · June 10, 2026

Legal News

Here's a number worth sitting with: San Diego PD has been classifying roughly 79 to 80 percent of all misconduct complaints filed against its officers as "miscellaneous." Here's the follow-up that should really get your attention — that category doesn't exist anywhere in the law that created the oversight body supposed to be reviewing those complaints.

San Diego voters passed Measure B in 2020, establishing the Commission on Police Practices as an independent body with a Charter-level mandate. The implementing Municipal Code language is about as unambiguous as legislative drafting gets: the Commission "must receive, register, review, and evaluate all complaints against police officers," with exactly two narrow exceptions — a complainant asking for no investigation, or no specific allegation or officer identified. "Miscellaneous" isn't one of them. It shows up nowhere in the Charter, nowhere in the Municipal Code, and exists only inside SDPD's own internal operations manual.

That gap is the entire premise of the writ of mandate filed by Pillars of the Community and advocate Tasha Williamson: an agency's internal administrative label cannot override a mandatory, voter-approved duty to send complaints to independent civilian review. The petition points to specific examples — including a complaint alleging officers used force against and falsely detained a group of minors — that were allegedly never forwarded to the Commission at all.

Whatever a court ultimately decides, the underlying legal argument here is clean and hard to dismiss with a wave of the hand: you don't get to write your own exception into a mandatory statute and then act like it was always there. Every California agency running a civilian oversight structure should be taking a hard look at whether its own internal complaint classifications are quietly doing something the governing ordinance never actually authorized.
