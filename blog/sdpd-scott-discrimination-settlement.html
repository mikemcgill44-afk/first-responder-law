Eleven Years, Three Lawsuits, One $3.1 Million Answer to 'Why Didn't You Just Let It Go?'

Legal News · Civil Rights · July 14, 2023

Legal News

Scott v. City of San Diego (San Diego County Superior Court) is the case in point. Nearly thirty years on the job, and Sergeant Arthur Scott spent more than a third of that career in litigation over what happened after he objected to racist material used in department trainings — images that included a demeaning caricature of the city's first Black police officer. The San Diego City Council just approved a $3.1 million settlement to close the book on it.

The timeline is a masterclass in how retaliation compounds over time. Scott's first complaint dates back to 2014. He filed his first lawsuit in 2015 and lost at trial in 2017. Rather than that being the end of it, subsequent years brought transfers, denied promotions, and — after Scott testified against the department in separate sexual harassment and excessive force cases — an internal investigation accusing him of lying under oath, followed by a termination notice. Scott filed a second lawsuit alleging retaliation, then a third challenging the termination itself.

The city's approved settlement resolves the two more recent suits, following a closed-session council vote and final approval. Scott's original 2015 case remains a matter of public record as a loss at trial and on appeal — a reminder that even a jury siding with the department once doesn't end a pattern that keeps generating new claims.

For risk managers and command staff, the real lesson isn't the dollar figure — it's the timeline. A single complaint about training materials, unresolved and allegedly met with retaliation instead of correction, metastasized into over a decade of litigation and a seven-figure payout. Whatever it costs to actually fix a problem the first time it's reported is almost always cheaper than what it costs to fight about it for eleven years.
