A Deputy Sued His Own Union for Failing to Represent Him. Here's Why PERB Dismissed the Case.

Legal News · PERB · April 1, 2026

Legal News

San Francisco Deputy Sheriffs' Association (Santana) (PERB Dec. No. 3014-M, March 27, 2026) addresses the flip side of labor representation: what happens when a member believes the union itself has failed them. Charging Party Andre Santana alleged the Association retaliated and discriminated against him and breached its duty of fair representation, in violation of the MMBA.

PERB's Office of the General Counsel dismissed the charge after review, and Santana appealed to the Board. In a non-precedential decision, the Board denied the appeal and affirmed the dismissal.

Duty-of-fair-representation claims against a union face a demanding standard under California law: the member has to show the union's conduct was arbitrary, discriminatory, or in bad faith — not merely that the union made a judgment call the member disagreed with. Ordinary tactical decisions in handling a grievance rarely clear that bar.

Deputies and officers considering a fair-representation claim against their own union should understand this threshold going in. A union's discretionary choices about how to pursue a grievance are given wide latitude, even when the outcome disappoints the member who filed it.
