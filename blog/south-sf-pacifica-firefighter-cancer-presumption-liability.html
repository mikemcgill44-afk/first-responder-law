He Got Cancer Firefighting for Two Cities — They Fought Over Who Pays, Not Whether He Deserved It

Legal News · Workers' Compensation · February 26, 2018

Legal News

Firefighter Richard Johnson worked first for the City of South San Francisco and later for the City of Pacifica before he developed nasopharyngeal cancer. Under Labor Code section 3212.1, California presumes that a firefighter's cancer arose out of the job — nobody disputed that Johnson's claim was valid. The fight, in City of South San Francisco v. Workers' Compensation Appeals Board (2018) 20 Cal.App.5th 881, was over which city had to pay for it.

South San Francisco settled Johnson's workers' compensation claim and then went after Pacifica for contribution, arguing Pacifica should share the cost since Johnson worked there too before his diagnosis. Under Labor Code section 5500.5, liability for a cumulative injury like cancer falls on whichever employer employed the firefighter during the one-year window before the injury date or the last date of injurious exposure. An arbitrator sided with Pacifica, finding the medical evidence on cancer latency showed Johnson's injurious exposure actually happened earlier, during his time with South San Francisco. The Workers' Compensation Appeals Board adopted that finding.

On appeal, South San Francisco argued the Board used the wrong evidentiary standard — that it should have applied the tougher standard used to rebut the cancer presumption itself, not an ordinary preponderance-of-the-evidence standard, when deciding which city was on the hook. The Court of Appeal disagreed and affirmed. The heightened rebuttal standard exists to protect firefighters from having their legitimate claims denied, the court explained — it has nothing to do with how two employers divide up liability once the claim is already accepted as valid. For firefighters who've worked cumulative-exposure jobs across multiple departments, this case is a reminder that even when your claim is never in doubt, the money can still get tied up in a fight between the agencies that employed you.
