Two Officers Were Indicted Over a Fatal Shooting Involving a Toy Gun. A Judge Just Ended the Case.

Legal News · Criminal Law · February 19, 2025

Legal News

The dismissal of the voluntary manslaughter case against two Torrance police officers (L.A. County Superior Court, 2025) closes a criminal prosecution that had already worked its way through a controversial indictment. The officers were charged in the 2018 shooting death of 23-year-old Christopher Deandre Mitchell, who was found in a suspected stolen vehicle with an altered air rifle between his legs.

The Los Angeles County District Attorney's Office itself requested the dismissal, telling the court it could not prove the case beyond a reasonable doubt — an unusual outcome for a prosecution that had already survived to the indictment stage. The case had drawn scrutiny for years over how the DA's office charges officer-involved shootings and how much deference grand juries extend to prosecutorial theories in those cases.

For officers, the case is a reminder that an indictment is not a conviction, and that criminal exposure in an on-duty shooting can drag on for years even when the ultimate outcome favors the officer. The gap between filing and resolution is where careers, finances, and reputations take the heaviest hits — regardless of how the case eventually ends.

Departments and unions watching officer-involved shooting prosecutions should take note of how thin the evidentiary margin can be even in cases that clear the charging threshold. If you're an officer under investigation following a shooting, get counsel involved immediately — the charging decision is not the finish line.
