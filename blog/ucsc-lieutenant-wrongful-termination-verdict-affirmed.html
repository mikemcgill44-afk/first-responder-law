Fired, Then Vindicated: A Record Verdict for a Wrongfully Terminated Police Lieutenant Survives Appeal

Legal News · Discipline · May 20, 2026

Legal News

The UC Santa Cruz lieutenant’s wrongful-termination verdict, affirmed on appeal (Cal. Ct. App., 6th Dist., 2026), proves the point. Agencies love to talk about accountability when it's pointed at an officer. This case is a reminder that accountability runs both directions, and when it points back at the agency, the number at the end can be brutal.

A UC Santa Cruz police lieutenant, wrongfully terminated, took the university to trial and walked away with a record verdict. The Court of Appeal has now affirmed it, closing off the university's shot at getting the number reduced or the finding reversed. The details of exactly how the termination unraveled at trial are less important than the headline number and what it represents: a jury looked at how this officer was treated, decided the termination didn't hold up, and priced that decision accordingly.

Wrongful termination verdicts against public safety employers tend to follow a pattern — a decision gets made under pressure, the internal process cuts corners, and by the time it reaches a jury, the paper trail tells a story the agency's attorneys can't spin their way out of. Juries do not love watching a long, clean service record get erased over a thin investigative record, and they especially don't love it when the agency's own process looks like it was built to reach a predetermined conclusion rather than find the truth.

For command staff and personnel attorneys reading this from the agency side: an affirmed record verdict like this one isn't just bad news for one university's budget. It's a data point every plaintiff's attorney representing a terminated officer is going to cite in the next settlement negotiation, and it raises the price of getting a termination decision wrong.

For officers facing what feels like a rigged process: this is exactly the kind of case that proves a wrongful termination doesn't have to be the end of the story.
