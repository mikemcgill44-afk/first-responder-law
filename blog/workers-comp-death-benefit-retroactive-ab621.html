Your Family's Workers' Comp Death Benefit Just Got a Major Upgrade — Retroactive to 2019

Legal News · Workers' Compensation · July 10, 2026

Legal News

California's workers' compensation death benefit rules have long contained a quiet trap for one group of safety members, and a new law just closed it — retroactively. Under Assembly Bill 621, which amends Labor Code section 4707, state safety members, peace officers, and firefighters employed by the Department of Forestry and Fire Protection are now exempt from a benefit-offset rule that used to apply to them, and the fix reaches back to cover injuries dating to January 1, 2019, for claims not already resolved.

Here's the trap the bill closes. Workers' compensation generally pays a death benefit when an employee dies from a work-related injury. But existing law caps that benefit at a mere $1,000 in burial expenses for an employee who was an active member of the Public Employees' Retirement System when they died — unless the PERS death benefit turns out to be smaller than the workers' comp death benefit would have been, in which case the surviving spouse and children are entitled to just the difference between the two. Local safety members and patrol members were already carved out of that limitation entirely, meaning their families could collect the full workers' comp death benefit on top of PERS survivor benefits. State safety members, state peace officers, and CAL FIRE firefighters were not.

AB 621 extends the same exemption those local safety and patrol members already had to state safety members, peace officers, and CAL FIRE firefighters — meaning their surviving families are no longer limited to the smaller of the two benefit amounts, or forced to accept a bare burial-expense payment because PERS coverage technically existed. Critically, the Legislature made the fix retroactive to January 1, 2019, for injuries not previously claimed or resolved, which means families of state safety members and CAL FIRE firefighters who died from a qualifying work-related injury on or after that date may now be entitled to benefits the old rule would have denied them.

If a line-of-duty death in your family involved a state safety member, a state peace officer, or a CAL FIRE firefighter, and the claim was ever limited or denied because of the PERS offset this bill eliminates, it is worth having that claim re-examined now — the retroactive window reaches back six years, and a claim that looked closed under the old rule may not be closed at all under this one.
